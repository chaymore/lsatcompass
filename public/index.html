How I'd study for the LSAT

Hi, I'm Caleb. I scored a 180 on the LSAT, and I built LSAT Compass to share what worked for me.

The LSAT isn't really a test of what you know. It tests whether your brain spots the pattern in each question and thinks about it the right way. The good news is that you can train that, and the training is simple.

  1. Take practice tests and drill questions. Every miss shows you a pattern you don't see yet.
  2. Figure out exactly why you missed it. Ask AI to explain the right answer and where your thinking went wrong. It's good enough now that you don't need a tutor or hours of videos for this.
  3. Write it down in a wrong answer journal. Seeing your misses in one place shows you which mistakes keep coming back.
  4. Go after your weak spots. Drill your weakest question types until they stop being weak. Then find the next one.
  5. Stay consistent. A little bit most days beats a big cram once a week.

Repeat that loop, and the right way of thinking becomes automatic. That's when your score really moves.

Your wrong answer journal

Log every question you miss, and every one you guessed on. Review it once a week. You'll see the same few mistakes come up again and again, and those are exactly what to work on.
